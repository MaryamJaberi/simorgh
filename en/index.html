A story of a city and a mafia

Par-e Simorgh

Imagine Iran today as a game of Mafia. Some are awake at night and know who each other are; the rest are awake by day and do not. The game began in 1979.

This collection sets that table — and behind every statement stands 76 recorded claims, each with a source and an evidence tier, which you can inspect line by line.

What this is and is not

Is

  • An open dataset of 76 claims, each with a source and a falsifying condition.
  • A structural analysis in the language of game theory and Iranian myth.
  • An action page defining a possible step for eighteen different social groups.
  • An audit log saying where it is weak.

Is not

  • Not a forecast. The cost tables are models, not predictions.
  • Not the statement of any party or faction; it carries no party emblem.
  • Not complete — much of the analysis predates the events of 2025–26. We say so plainly in the audit.
  • Not neutral — but inspectable. Those are not the same thing.

Par-e Simorgh — a collection about Iran today, told as a game and a myth.

No party, state or religious emblem appears anywhere here; every symbol is drawn from the Shahnameh and Iranian myth. No quotation is attributed to a real person without a source. Cost figures are models, not forecasts.
