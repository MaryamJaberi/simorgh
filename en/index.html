The story of a city and a mafia

Par-e Simorgh

Suppose Iran today is a game of Mafia. Some players are awake at night and know who each other are. The rest are awake by day and do not. The game began in 1979, and seven nights have been played.

This collection sets out that table in full — the rules, the players, the nights already played — and then does something a Mafia game never does: it turns your own card face up and tells you what, tonight, can actually be done with it.

What this is and is not

Before you read, know what you are dealing with.

It is

  • An analytical frame in the language of a game and a myth — so that something complicated becomes sayable.
  • Sourced. Every figure and quotation has a source, and the Library flags the works that contradict this document as well as those that support it.
  • Open to criticism. The full Persian text includes a “table of critics” in which nine factions attack this document directly.
  • Free, with no tracker, no cookie and no analytics. Nothing about you is recorded.

It is not

  • Not a prediction. No date of collapse is given anywhere. The cost figures are models, not forecasts.
  • Not a party platform. No party, state or religious emblem is used; every symbol comes from the Shahnameh.
  • Not a set of instructions. Your Card reports what the research found, not what you should do — and it includes a card for people who would rather not act at all.
  • Not finished. Much of the analysis was written before the events of 2025–2026 and has not yet been revised.
