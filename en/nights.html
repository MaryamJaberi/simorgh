One pattern, before the details

If only one thing survives from this step, let it be this: the interval between waves keeps getting shorter. Seventy-two years separated the Constitutional Revolution from 1979. From 2017 to now: four waves in eight years.

Each wave, taken alone, “failed”. But something is accumulating — and that accumulation is what Kadivar's research says durable democracy is actually built from, rather than from one large explosion.

The rounds, in order

Each entry is a summary. The full analysis of every round, with its corresponding Shahnameh myth, is in the Persian second chapter.

The mafia was chosen by the city's own vote

The first round. A broad coalition removed the previous structure — and then one partner in that coalition removed the others from the table. The lasting point for today: uniting to say “no” is not by itself any guarantee about what comes next.

Consolidation through war, and the making of new roles

The eight-year war consolidated the new structure and built institutions that outlived the war itself. This is where many of the roles now sitting at the table were born.

The city tried to vote from inside the game

An attempt at change through the system's own channels, with high turnout and broad hope. Structural limits then showed the ceiling of what was possible from within.

The “reform” card burned

The decisive round. Once the internal route closes, that card can no longer be played in later rounds — and that is a cost still being paid.

The bread breaking point

Waves whose spark was economic and which turned political very quickly, and which moved the geography of protest from the centre to everywhere. From here on, the composition of the street is not what it was.

The roles were redefined

The wave in which women took the central role and customary boundaries moved. Part of the change — as the research shows — stayed in the household rather than in the law. Which is itself a kind of result.

The night without precedent

The most recent round, with an external component — something no earlier round had. The top of the structure was damaged, decision-making was disrupted, and pressure on ordinary people rose at the same time. This round is not over.

The situation changes daily. For a sourced account see Iranians & Israelis and the Library.

And today: the window is open, the process is not built

This is the most important sentence on this step

Across all seven rounds one pattern repeats: moments when the table shook were not scarce — what was missing was a mechanism to turn such a moment into an outcome.

The window has opened many times. The process that could pass through it has not been built. That is why Your Card matters more than any analysis of the past.
