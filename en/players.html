Three circles

The first rule on the previous step said there are three factions, not two. Here each one opens up.

The mafia — those awake at night

Those who know one another, share a fate, and whose survival is tied to the survival of the structure. The important point: this is not a uniform bloc. There is rivalry, fear and fracture inside it — and the transition literature says it is precisely that internal fracture that has proved decisive in most transitions.

For the research behind this, see Svolik and Bueno de Mesquita in the Library.

The city — those awake by day

The majority. Their problem is not that they are few; it is that they do not know each other. Each assumes they are alone, so each stays cautious, so everyone else also concludes they are alone.

The city is not uniform either: a shopkeeper, a worker, a teacher and a civil servant each hold a different lever. Your Card separates exactly this out — eighteen different cards for eighteen different positions.

The independents — those playing for themselves

Neither with the mafia nor with the city. They pursue their own interests and can go either way — much of the bazaar, parts of the bureaucracy, parts of the armed forces, regional and international actors.

Most analyses omit this circle. Yet in the history of transitions, the moment the independents turn is the moment the table changes.

And where are you?

Nobody sits in only one circle. A civil servant is both the city and part of the structure. A shopkeeper is both the city and an independent. The Venn diagram exists for exactly this reason: seven overlapping regions, not three closed boxes.

A note on method

A role is not a person

Throughout this collection, roles are positions, not individuals. None of the 37 or 47 roles is attributed to a named real person, and no quotation is attached to anyone without a source.

The reason is not only caution: if you mistake the role for the person, the analysis goes wrong. Removing a person does not remove the role — exactly as the fourth rule said.
