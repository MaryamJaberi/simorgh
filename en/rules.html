Why a game?

Because in Mafia, everyone knows there is a mafia. What they do not know is which of them it is. That one small difference changes the whole behaviour of the table: people grow suspicious of each other, they vote against each other, and when night falls they are alone.

This is precisely what the research on closed systems describes, and on Your Card we give it its academic name: preference falsification. In Mafia it is called bluffing. In sociology it is called the reason regimes fall suddenly.

The game is not a literary flourish. It is a precise instrument for discussing something that is hard to say directly — and for letting someone who has never read a book of political science enter the argument anyway.

Four rules of this table

These are summaries. The full text, with examples and exceptions, is the first chapter of the Persian game.

Three factions, not two

The mafia is awake at night and knows who the others are. The city is awake by day and does not. And the independents — who play for themselves and may go either way.

Most analyses drop the third faction and are then surprised when their predictions fail. The next step opens all three circles properly.

One vote, on one day, for one thing

The city only wins when it converges on a single day, on a single thing. A hundred scattered votes across a hundred options are worse than no vote at all, because they demonstrate to the table that the city is not coordinated.

This is the core of every argument about why oppositions fail to convert pressure into outcome.

Violence is inversely proportional

The harder it is to enter the game, the fewer players there are; and the fewer players, the lower the city's chance of winning. This is not a moral claim, it is arithmetic — and the figures come from 323 historical uprisings.

Your grandmother can join a strike. She cannot join an armed confrontation. That single sentence determines most of the strategy.

The game does not end when one player dies

In Mafia, removing a player does not end the round; the structure is what keeps the game going. Remove a role while the structure stands, and a new role simply sits down in the same chair.

This is why the collection talks about roles rather than individuals — and why “after the fall” is the most urgent section of the Library.

And one rule that does not belong to the game

The metaphor breaks in one place

In Mafia, nobody really dies. Here they do. The game lets us see the structure; it does not license us to treat the cost lightly.

Wherever you see a figure about cost on this site, that figure is a model and not a forecast — and wherever a card is offered to you, read the risk section before it.
